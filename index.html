Key Ingredients

Three ingredients. One patented formula.

Use the current official label to confirm the full formula. Ingredient research provides context but does not automatically prove an outcome from the finished gel.

Carnosine
Ingredient context

Carnosine is found naturally in muscle and is studied in exercise physiology. Confirm whether any cited research used LactiGo's finished topical formula, not only oral supplementation or laboratory models.

  • Check the concentration and full formula
  • Check the delivery route used in each study
  • Check participant number and comparison group
  • Check whether results were independently replicated
Magnesium
Formulation ingredient

Magnesium has important physiological roles, but that does not establish that topical application prevents cramps or fatigue. Assess the evidence for this exact formulation.

  • Do not infer topical absorption from dietary evidence
  • Review the supplied use directions
  • Ask for finished-product substantiation
Menthol
Cooling sensation

Menthol is commonly used for a cooling feeling on the skin. Sensory relief does not diagnose or resolve the cause of persistent pain.

  • Follow current external-use directions
  • Avoid excluded areas listed on the label
  • Stop use if irritation develops

Healthy Aging

Staying active as you age.
Keep the evidence specific.

Age can influence exercise capacity, recovery and muscle physiology, but it is too simplistic to attribute those changes to one molecule or claim that one topical product reverses them.

When research discusses carnosine and ageing, check the intervention and route carefully. Evidence involving diet or oral beta-alanine does not automatically establish the effect of LactiGo's finished topical formula.

📉

Ageing Is Multifactorial

Training history, sleep, nutrition, health and recovery habits all matter. Avoid reducing performance changes to a single cause.

🔬

Match Evidence to the Product

Look for human research on the exact topical formulation, relevant participants and an appropriate comparison group.

🏃

Use It as a Topical

Follow the current external-use label. “Nothing to ingest” does not mean no one can experience irritation or another unwanted effect.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is carnosine gel and how is it different from other products?
LactiGo is a topical gel formulated with carnosine and marketed for use around exercise. The manufacturer references US Patent 10,973,868. A patent protects an invention; it does not by itself prove a clinical outcome. Review the current label and finished-product research.
When should I apply LactiGo?
Follow the directions on the exact product supplied to you, as formats and labels can change. Do not rely on an older website or social post in place of the package directions.
Is carnosine gel better than beta-alanine or oral carnosine supplements?
They are different products and the available research does not establish that one option is best for everyone. Beta-alanine, oral supplements and topical products have different evidence, dosing and safety considerations. Review the published research and ask a qualified sports-health professional which approach fits your needs.
Can carnosine gel help with healthy aging?
General research about carnosine, ageing or oral supplementation does not automatically show that a topical finished product improves exercise capacity. Look for research that tests LactiGo itself in the relevant age group.
Is LactiGo safe for drug-tested athletes?
The manufacturer states that LactiGo is Informed Sport certified. Drug-tested athletes should verify the exact current product or batch in the official Informed Sport directory and follow their sport's governing rules.
What is the return policy?

What sports is LactiGo used in?
